Only one salesperson does the drawings. The other sales staff pass their orders to her, so requests pile up at her desk. Many client changes are small, and one client went through ten versions before returning to the first. She hoped AI could take over this simple but repeated work.

In our interviews she worried about two things. How would her work change, and would the AI's output be good enough? She mentioned that AI could maybe handle the middle steps while she stayed at the end. It was an idea, not a request. I designed the flow from it. For machine types the model has learned, AI makes the base drawing and revises it to the client's needs. She confirms at the last step whether the drawing can be delivered. I drew the process map and marked each step AI would take over.

Engineers built most of the prototype and connected a real AI model. I helped them clarify the features, and I checked whether the finished prototype met the client's needs. The prototype could revise a drawing from one sentence of feedback, though the first run on each machine type was slow. She walked through the prototype with her real cases, and I also checked with her how she and the other sales staff hand work to each other.
